# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The person who must leave satisfied is a **practitioner or developer** in a development organization, a research group, or a local AI team, who arrives looking for a dataset, model, or use case they can actually reuse in their own work. Their context is task-driven: they scan, filter by domain or country, open a detail panel, and follow through to the source. The catalog's job is to get them to that reusable asset with as little friction as possible.

Two secondary audiences matter and must not be designed away. **Policymakers and funders** (including BMZ stakeholders) survey the catalog to understand what open AI building blocks exist across international development and to gauge the initiative's breadth; the hero and stats strip largely speak to them. **Partner organizations** contribute the data and want their projects discovered, credited, and reused.

Note a surface-vs-user split: the site UI serves consumers of the data, while contribution happens off-site through the source Google Sheet and a GitHub Actions build, not through the interface. The catalog is a read-and-reuse surface, not an authoring tool.

## Product Purpose

An open catalog of datasets and AI use cases for international development, maintained by the FAIR Forward initiative (part of BMZ Digital). It exists so that AI work built by local partners does not stay siloed: the assets are gathered, verified, and made discoverable in one place so others can reuse them. Success, in order of value, is a visitor who finds and reuses an open asset; a visitor who comes away understanding the landscape of what exists; and a visitor who makes a connection, reaching a partner or contributing data.

## Positioning

Reusable, open AI building blocks for global development challenges, built by local partners. Every screen should reinforce that what is listed here is not a showcase of finished products to admire but a working supply of datasets, models, and use cases to pick up and build on.

## Brand Personality

Credible and trustworthy first: the catalog reads as vetted and accurate, data you can cite and rely on. Open and inviting: it welcomes newcomers and contributors, signalling that the work is shared and reusable rather than gated. Quietly optimistic: it conveys momentum and possibility about locally-built open AI without hype or savior framing. The voice is plain, precise, and free of jargon; the interface should feel like a well-kept public resource, not a sales pitch or a charity appeal.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate SaaS pitch**: no gradient hero, big-metric template, or buy-now startup landing framing. This is a public good, not a product for sale.
- **Aid-org stock imagery**: no beneficiary stock photos, savior framing, or emotive charity visuals.
- **Dense bureaucratic data portal**: not the gray government open-data-portal look with cramped tables and no hierarchy.
- **Trendy AI aesthetic**: no neon-on-black, glowing orbs, or sci-fi spectacle. The AI here is practical infrastructure.
- **Generic AI-generated page**: explicitly not the templated look of a quickly-generated site, over-dense information, default fonts, and cookie-cutter buttons, widgets, and boxed card grids. Distinction is earned through craft, not assembled from defaults.

## Design Principles

- **Verified before visible.** Nothing appears in the catalog unless it is accurate. Trust is the core asset, and the interface should never surface unchecked or placeholder information as if it were confirmed.
- **The asset is the destination.** The shortest honest path from search to a reusable dataset wins. The tool gets out of the way of the task; every extra step between intent and source is a cost.
- **Credit the makers.** Surface who built each thing and where it comes from. The value proposition is that these are locally built and openly shared, so authorship and provenance are features, not footnotes.
- **Impressive without selling.** The showcase framing can inspire a funder or a newcomer, but never at the expense of the practitioner's task, and never through SaaS theatrics or charity emotion.
- **Distinct, not templated.** Reject the generic generated-page look. Familiarity is fine where it serves the task; sameness assembled from defaults is not.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal standard is mandated, but accessibility is treated as a house standard and must not regress. Keep and extend the affordances already in the code: skip links, visible focus outlines, reduced-motion support, semantic landmarks, and screen-reader labels. Hold body text to at least 4.5:1 contrast and keep the catalog fully keyboard operable, aiming at WCAG 2.1 AA in spirit even without certification.
